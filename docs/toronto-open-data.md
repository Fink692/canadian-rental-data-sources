# Toronto renter research with open data

Toronto's Open Data Portal publishes datasets that can help a renter ask better questions before signing. The datasets should be read alongside direct viewing, written lease terms, RentSafeTO guidance, and the province's tenancy rules.

## 1. Apartment Building Registration

Source: [Apartment Building Registration](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/apartment-building-registration/)

Useful question: Is this address part of the City's registered apartment-building inventory, and what building-level identifiers are available for cross-checking?

Important limitation: Registration does not cover every kind of rental or prove a unit's current condition, legality, or availability.

## 2. Apartment Building Evaluation

Source: [Apartment Building Evaluation](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/apartment-building-evaluation/)

Useful question: What building-level RentSafeTO evaluation record is available, and when was it recorded?

Important limitation: The dataset applies to registered apartment buildings. A building-level evaluation is not a fresh inspection of a specific unit and should not be treated as a guarantee.

## 3. Building Permits — Active Permits

Source: [Building Permits — Active Permits](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/building-permits-active-permits/)

Useful question: Does the address appear in active permit records, and what type of work or administrative status is described?

Important limitation: A permit record documents a municipal process. It does not prove that work is complete, defect-free, or that a unit is presently habitable.

## 4. Road Restrictions

Source: [Road Restrictions](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/road-restrictions/)

Useful question: Are current or planned restrictions likely to affect the route around the address?

Important limitation: Dates and impacts can change, and the dataset does not capture every private or short-notice disruption.

## A careful workflow

1. Confirm the exact civic address and unit from the listing and in person.
2. Search registration and evaluation data by the fields the source supports.
3. Check active permits and read the work description and status without inferring completion.
4. Check road restrictions for near-term route context.
5. Record the source URL and the date checked.
6. Ask the landlord or property manager specific written questions about anything material.
7. Verify tenancy-law questions through [Ontario's renter-rights page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/renting-ontario-your-rights) or the [Landlord and Tenant Board](https://tribunalsontario.ca/ltb/).

This guide enables discovery and careful interpretation of City of Toronto open datasets. It does not republish the dynamic records, rate a neighbourhood, or provide legal or safety advice.
