# Canadian Rental Address Research

An open, source-dated directory of official Canadian resources for researching a rental address before signing a lease. It starts with sixteen cities: Vancouver, Victoria, Surrey, Calgary, Edmonton, Saskatoon, Regina, Winnipeg, Toronto, Mississauga, Brampton, Hamilton, Ottawa, Montréal, Québec City, and Halifax.

**Français — Québec :** [Rechercher une adresse locative à Québec avec des sources officielles](docs/ville-de-quebec-sources-officielles.md).

This is a research checklist, not a rating system. It does **not** rate personal safety, verify a property's physical condition, give legal advice, or guarantee that a home is suitable.

## A practical pre-lease checklist

### 1. Match the exact address

- Compare the civic address and unit number in the listing, lease draft, municipal map, and what you see in person.
- Make sure a basement, laneway home, or subdivided unit appears consistent with available zoning, permit, and occupancy records.
- Treat a missing public record as a reason to ask questions—not proof that a unit is illegal.

### 2. Verify who is offering the unit

- Ask the advertiser to identify the legal owner or the property manager acting for the owner.
- Confirm the property through an official assessment, parcel, or land-title source where available.
- Remember that an authorized manager may lawfully rent a property even when their name is not on title.
- Never rely on a screenshot supplied by the advertiser when you can open the official source independently.

### 3. Review permits, orders, and building records

- Search the address for development or building permits, property-standard records, or rental-building evaluations available in that city.
- Read status and dates carefully. A permit can show that work was approved or applied for; it does not prove present habitability or workmanship.
- Ask in writing about unresolved repairs, water intrusion, pests, heating, electrical work, and planned construction.

### 4. Check the lease against provincial rules

- Use the province's tenancy authority—not a social post—to check deposits, rent increases, entry, repairs, notices, and dispute routes.
- Read every page before paying. Save the listing, lease, receipts, inspection photos, and written promises.
- If the facts are unclear or the stakes are high, contact the relevant tenancy authority or a qualified legal professional.

### 5. Test daily life yourself

- Visit at more than one time of day when possible.
- Test the real commute, late-night transit availability, mobile signal, water pressure, noise, lighting, accessibility, and nearby essentials.
- Check flood, construction, traffic, transit, and police data only as context. Data coverage and reporting practices vary.

### 6. Slow down when payment pressure appears

- Do not send a deposit merely to reserve an unseen unit.
- Independently verify the address, advertiser authority, written lease terms, payment recipient, and refund conditions.
- Be cautious when someone refuses a viewing, creates artificial urgency, asks for unusual payment methods, or wants money before basic verification.

The Government of Canada's [first-apartment renting guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/renting-first-apartment.html) is a useful national starting point.

## Registered ownership sources

Municipal assessment and parcel records help confirm that an address exists, but the provincial land registry is the authoritative source for registered ownership and interests. Access commonly requires a fee, subscription, parcel identifier, or registry agent. A title name still does not prove that a specific advertiser is authorized; ask a non-owner or property manager for written authority.

| Coverage | Official access point | Access note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| British Columbia | [LTSA title search guidance](https://ltsa.ca/property-owners/how-can-i/search-for-a-title/) | Fee; normally requires the parcel identifier (PID). |
| Alberta | [Land titles documents and plans](https://www.alberta.ca/find-land-titles-documents-plans.aspx) | Fee; available through ARLO, SPIN2, or a registry agent. |
| Manitoba | [Registrar-General / Property Registry](https://www.gov.mb.ca/registrar-general/) | The provincial page explains registry oversight and links to the service provider. |
| Ontario | [Land registry overview and OnLand access](https://www.ontario.ca/page/overview-land-registry) | Self-search is available online for a fee. |
| Québec | [Québec Land Register](https://www.quebec.ca/en/housing-territory/land-information/land-register) | French online register; consultation fees apply and the lot number is useful. |
| Nova Scotia | [Property Online](https://www.novascotia.ca/sign-property-online) | Subscription service for ownership and land-registry maps. |

## National market context

- [CMHC rental market data](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-data/data-tables/rental-market) provides vacancy and rent statistics for Canadian markets.
- [Statistics Canada's Census Profile](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/index-eng.cfm) provides population, housing, commuting, and household context.
- [Government of Canada Open Maps](https://search.open.canada.ca/openmap/) helps find federal geospatial datasets.

These sources describe markets or areas. They do not validate a particular listing or predict a future experience.

## How to interpret a record

| A source may help show | It does not prove |
| --- | --- |
| An address exists in a municipal system | The listing is legitimate or the advertiser is authorized |
| A permit was applied for, issued, or closed | The work is defect-free or the unit is currently habitable |
| A building received an evaluation | Every unit has the same conditions today |
| A property is associated with a named owner | The owner is the advertiser, or that no authorized manager exists |
| Reported incidents occurred in an area | That a person or property is safe or unsafe |
| Typical market rent or vacancy | The fair or lawful rent for a particular unit |

## Use the directory locally

The repository has no runtime dependencies. Clone it, then open `README.md` in a browser or `data/sources.csv` in a spreadsheet program:

```sh
git clone https://github.com/Fink692/canadian-rental-data-sources.git
cd canadian-rental-data-sources
```

Python 3 contributors can run the dependency-free structural check before proposing a source change:

```sh
python scripts/validate_sources.py
```

The validator checks the CSV schema, required values, URL shape, ISO source-check dates, and accidental duplicate rows. It does not contact external sites; contributors must still open each official source and verify it manually.

## Limitations

Public records can be incomplete, delayed, corrected, removed, or scoped differently across cities. This directory does not reproduce source data and does not endorse conclusions about residents, owners, neighbourhoods, or protected groups. Always open the source, read its date and methodology, and verify important facts directly.
